CYBERSECURITY AWARENESS

How to Recognise Phishing Emails and Online Scams

Fraudulent emails, text messages and websites can look convincing. Learning to recognise warning signs and verify requests safely can help protect your personal information, accounts and business data.

← Back to IT Advice & Guides

What is phishing?

Phishing is a form of fraud in which criminals impersonate trusted organisations or individuals to trick people into sharing information, transferring money or installing malicious software.

Phishing attempts may arrive through email, text messages, social media, messaging applications or fraudulent websites. Some messages appear to come from banks, delivery companies, government services or familiar businesses.

Seven warning signs to look for

  1. Unexpected urgency: The message pressures you to act immediately to avoid account suspension, penalties or financial loss.
  2. Requests for sensitive information: You are asked to provide passwords, verification codes, payment details or other confidential information.
  3. Suspicious links: A link leads to an unfamiliar or misleading website address, even though the message looks legitimate.
  4. Unexpected attachments: An unsolicited document or file asks you to enable macros, install software or bypass security warnings.
  5. Unusual payment instructions: A supplier or contact unexpectedly requests payment to a different bank account.
  6. Impersonation: A message claims to come from someone you trust, but the request or communication method is unusual.
  7. Offers that seem too good to be true: Unexpected prizes, refunds or investment opportunities require payment or personal information.

A message can still be fraudulent even when it contains correct spelling, professional branding or personal details. No single warning sign proves that a message is genuine or fraudulent.

How to verify a suspicious message safely

  1. Do not rush. Avoid clicking links, opening attachments or making payments until you have checked the request.
  2. Use an independent contact method. Visit the organisation's official website by entering its address yourself, or use a previously verified telephone number.
  3. Check payment changes separately. Confirm new bank details with the supplier through an established, trusted communication channel.
  4. Protect your accounts. Use unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication wherever available.
  5. Report suspicious messages. Use your email provider's reporting feature or an appropriate official reporting service.

What should you do if you clicked a suspicious link?

Clicking a suspicious link does not necessarily mean your device or accounts have been compromised. The appropriate response depends on what happened next.

  • If you entered a password, change it promptly using the genuine website or application. Change reused passwords on other accounts too.
  • If you disclosed banking information or transferred money, contact your bank immediately through its official fraud-reporting channel.
  • If you downloaded or ran suspicious software, stop using the affected device for sensitive activities and seek appropriate technical assistance.
  • If a work account or business device is involved, report the incident promptly to the responsible administrator or IT support provider.
  • Review account activity and security settings, and follow the relevant provider's account recovery instructions.

Protecting your small business

Small businesses may encounter fraudulent invoices, supplier impersonation, fake account notifications and attempts to obtain access to company email.

Establish clear procedures for verifying payment changes, use multi-factor authentication, keep software updated and ensure important business information is backed up.

Encourage staff to report suspicious messages without delay. Early reporting can help reduce the impact of an attempted scam.

Where to report suspicious messages

In the UK, suspicious emails can be forwarded to the National Cyber Security Centre at report@phishing.gov.uk. Suspicious text messages can generally be forwarded to 7726.

If you have lost money or disclosed sensitive information, contact your bank or relevant service provider immediately and report the fraud through the appropriate official UK reporting service.

Visit the National Cyber Security Centre's phishing guidance for further information.
